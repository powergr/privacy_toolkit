Paranoid Mode: Harvesting Entropy from Human Movement

Published on November 15, 2025 by The Project QRE Team

In the world of cryptography, randomness (entropy) is the fuel that powers everything. If an attacker can predict your "random" numbers, they can predict your keys.

Standard computers use Pseudo-Random Number Generators (PRNGs). These are generally secure for most uses, but they rely on the operating system's seed. What if the OS is compromised? What if a sophisticated AI detects a pattern in the CPU's timing?

The Solution: Biological Entropy

With QRE Locker, we introduced a feature designed for users who cannot afford to trust standard algorithms alone: Paranoid Mode.

Instead of relying solely on the computer's internal clock or kernel noise, Paranoid Mode injects entropy from the most unpredictable source available: You.

How It Works

When Paranoid Mode is enabled in the Advanced settings, QRE Locker activates a specialized collection algorithm during the key generation process:

  • Mouse Movements: We track the precise X/Y coordinates of your mouse cursor.
  • Micro-Timing: We capture the exact nanosecond intervals between input events.

Human hand movements are chaotic. The microscopic jitters and inconsistent speed variations create a data stream that is statistically unique every single time.

The Implementation

In our Rust backend, we take this "biological noise" and feed it into a SHA-256 hasher. This digest is then used to seed a ChaCha20 cryptographically secure random number generator.

Crucially, we do not replace the system's entropy; we mix into it. Even if your hand doesn't move, the system defaults back to the standard OS security. But if you do move, you add a layer of randomness that exists outside the mathematical realm of the CPU.

For 99% of users, the standard mode is secure. For the 1% who face adversaries with unlimited resources, Paranoid Mode provides that extra layer of physical unpredictability.
