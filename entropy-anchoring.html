Entropy Anchoring: Beyond Passwords

In a typical encryption tool, security relies entirely on your password. If your password is weak, your data is vulnerable. QRE Locker solves this with Entropy Anchoring, a technique that binds your encryption key to a physical digital artifact (a "Keyfile") in addition to your passphrase.

1. The "Something You Have" Factor

Passwords are "something you know." A Keyfile is "something you have." QRE Locker allows you to select any file on your computer—a photo of your dog, an MP3 song, a random PDF—and use it as a second factor for encryption.

To an attacker, this file is just one of millions on your hard drive. But to QRE Locker, it is the missing piece of the puzzle required to unlock your Master Key.

2. Mathematical Implementation (Argon2id)

We don't just "check" if the file is present. We cryptographically mix it into the key derivation process.

  • The Keyfile is hashed using SHA-256 to create a uniform entropy block.
  • This hash is combined with your Passphrase bytes.
  • The combined data is fed into Argon2id, a memory-hard key derivation function.

The result is a Master Key that cannot be derived without both the correct password and the exact bit-perfect copy of the Keyfile.

3. Defense Against Brute Force

This approach renders traditional brute-force attacks nearly impossible. Even if an attacker knows your password is 8 characters long, they cannot crack the file because they do not have the 5MB image file you used as an anchor.

The search space expands from "billions of passwords" to "every possible combination of bytes in every file on your computer."

4. Offline & Decentralized

Early versions of QRE explored using online chaotic data (like solar flares). However, for v2.0, we prioritized reliability and privacy. By using local Keyfiles, QRE Locker works perfectly without an internet connection, and you never have to trust an external server to provide your entropy. You own the anchor.
