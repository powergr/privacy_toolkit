Paranoid Mode: Injecting Chaos into Cryptography

Standard encryption tools rely entirely on the Operating System's random number generator (RNG). While generally secure, what if the OS itself is compromised, or its entropy pool is predictable? QRE Locker introduces Paranoid Mode to solve this.

1. The Problem with "Standard" Randomness

Encryption keys are just random numbers. If an attacker can guess the random number generator's state at the moment you clicked "Lock," they can predict your key. This is a theoretical but high-stakes attack vector, especially in virtualized environments or compromised systems.

2. Our Solution: User-Driven Entropy

When you check the "Paranoid Mode" box in QRE Locker v2.0, we stop trusting the computer to be random on its own. We bring you into the loop.

The application begins harvesting "Hardware Events" directly from your physical interactions with the machine. Specifically, we capture the microsecond-level timing of your mouse movements and system interrupts.

3. How It Works Under the Hood

The technical flow of Paranoid Mode is unique to QRE Locker:

  1. Harvesting: The browser engine (Frontend) collects a buffer of cryptographic randomness generated from your immediate hardware session.
  2. Injection: This "Chaos Buffer" is passed down to the Rust backend during the locking process.
  3. Mixing: We initialize a ChaCha20 stream cipher, but instead of seeding it with just OS data, we mix in your Chaos Buffer using a SHA-256 hash.
  4. Generation: This hybrid seed is then used to generate the critical Nonces (IVs) and Salts used for the file encryption.

4. Why This Matters

By injecting external entropy that is physically tied to the real world (your hand movements), we break the deterministic chain of the computer's internal state. Even if an attacker has a perfect snapshot of your computer's memory the moment before you encrypt, they cannot predict the exact chaotic data you introduced during the process.

It is an optional "Extra Layer" for those who demand the mathematical maximum of unpredictability.
